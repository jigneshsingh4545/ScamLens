    LEARN / PHISHING BASICS

    Recognize the hook.
    Choose your next step.

    Phishing is a deceptive message or call designed to get you to reveal information, send money, or open a harmful link or file. It can arrive by email, text, social media, or phone.

    01 WHAT TO EXPECT

    It often follows a familiar pattern

    1. 01
      A believable reason to contact you

      A delivery issue, account alert, invoice, shared document, job offer, or message that appears to come from someone you know.

    2. 02
      A reason to act or react

      The sender may introduce a deadline, a threat, a surprise reward, a problem to fix, or a request to keep things secret.

    3. 03
      A request that benefits the sender

      You may be asked to open a link or attachment, sign in, share a one-time code, provide personal details, or pay in an unusual way.

    02 CLUES TO CHECK

    Look at the whole request

    • Sender: Does the address or number match what you already have on file? Display names can be copied.
    • Link: Does the real domain match the organization? Don’t open it just to find out; navigate independently instead.
    • Ask: Is it requesting a password, one-time code, payment, or information it should not need?
    • Pressure: Is fear, urgency, a reward, or secrecy being used to rush your decision?
    ✳

    03 WHAT TO DO

    Pause, then verify another way.

    Don’t reply, click, open an attachment, or share a code while unsure. Contact the person or organization using a number, app, or website you already trust.

    If you already shared a password or payment details, contact the affected provider or bank directly and change credentials from its official site.

    A clue is not proof. Legitimate messages can look unusual, and convincing scams can look ordinary.

    AFTER A SCAM / TAKE THESE STEPS

    Act quickly.
    You still have options.

    Start with the steps that fit what happened. You don’t have to handle everything at once.

    1. 01

      Stop contact and don’t pay more

      Stop replying, sending money, or sharing codes. Don’t trust anyone who promises to recover your money for an upfront fee; that may be another scam.

    2. 02

      Contact your bank or payment provider

      If you sent money or shared payment details, contact the bank, card issuer, payment app, or wire service using its official app or the number on your card. Ask whether they can stop, reverse, or secure the transaction.

    3. 03

      Secure affected accounts

      From the official website or app, change any exposed password and any reused passwords. Turn on multi-factor authentication, sign out other sessions, and contact the account provider if you can’t get in. Never share a one-time code.

    4. 04

      Protect your device and identity

      If you opened an attachment or installed something, disconnect the device from sensitive accounts and run its trusted security tools. If identity details were exposed, contact the relevant government identity or credit-protection service in your country.

    5. 05

      Save evidence and report it

      Keep messages, receipts, transaction IDs, usernames, and dates. Report the incident to the platform involved and your local official fraud or consumer-protection agency. Use contact information you find independently.

    ✳

    KEEP IN MIND

    You are not alone, and it is not your fault.

    Scams are designed to manipulate people. Acting promptly can help limit further harm, but recovery is not always possible and no tool can guarantee an outcome.

    Make the next step clearer.

    Scamlens helps people take a breath and look for clues when a message, email, or link doesn’t feel right.
